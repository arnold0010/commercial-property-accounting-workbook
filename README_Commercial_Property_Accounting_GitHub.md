# Commercial Property Accounting & Financial Reporting Workbook

**Portfolio project \| Excel \| Accrual accounting \| Simulated US
commercial real estate**

A formula-driven accounting simulation for a fictional two-property
commercial real estate LLC. The workbook demonstrates the accounting
cycle from transaction capture and double-entry journals through the
general ledger, trial balance, adjusting entries, bank reconciliation,
and portfolio-level financial reporting.

> **Important:** This is an independently developed educational
> simulation. All entity names, tenants, lenders, bank details,
> transactions, and financial figures are fictional. It is not a client
> engagement, an audited set of financial statements, or a
> representation of any real company.

## Project objectives

The project was designed to demonstrate practical understanding of:

-   Double-entry bookkeeping and journal posting
-   Accrual accounting and period-end adjustments
-   General ledger maintenance and trial balance validation
-   Tenant receivables and security deposit liabilities
-   Prepaid insurance and accrued operating expenses
-   Mortgage interest and principal accounting
-   Bank reconciliation
-   Property-level and portfolio-level financial reporting
-   Owner contributions and distributions as equity transactions
-   Spreadsheet-based accounting controls and formula linkages

## Workbook structure

The Excel workbook is organised into 11 worksheets:

1.  **Company Profile & Chart of Accounts** --- entity assumptions,
    account codes and accounting structure.
2.  **Transaction Register** --- source-level operating and financing
    activity.
3.  **Journal Entries** --- debit and credit postings generated from
    transaction data.
4.  **General Ledger** --- account-level posting detail and balances.
5.  **Trial Balance** --- debit and credit balance verification.
6.  **Adjusting Entries** --- period-end accruals, prepayments,
    depreciation and bank-related adjustments.
7.  **Bank Reconciliation** --- reconciliation of book cash to the bank
    statement.
8.  **Profit & Loss Statement** --- property revenue, operating
    expenses, depreciation, interest and net income.
9.  **Balance Sheet** --- assets, liabilities and equity, including
    current and long-term mortgage presentation.
10. **Cash Flow Statement** --- operating, investing and financing cash
    flows.
11. **Management Financial Package** --- portfolio-level and
    property-level reporting metrics.

## Accounting scenarios demonstrated

### Revenue and accruals

Rental income is recognised when earned under the simulation's accrual
policy, rather than only when cash is received. December rent remains in
receivables at year-end when collection occurs in the following period.

### Prepaid insurance

The annual insurance premium is paid in advance. The unexpired portion
is retained as a prepaid asset, while the portion relating to the
reporting period is recognised as insurance expense.

### Accrued expenses

The model includes year-end accruals for property tax, fourth-quarter
operating costs and December mortgage interest that had not yet been
paid at the reporting date.

### Mortgage accounting

Mortgage instalments are separated into principal and interest. The
revised balance sheet presentation distinguishes the principal due
within the next 12 months from the remaining long-term mortgage balance.

### Tenant security deposits

Refundable tenant security deposits are treated as liabilities rather
than rental revenue.

### Owner contributions and distributions

Owner capital contributions are recorded in equity and classified as
financing cash flows. Owner distributions reduce equity; they are not
operating expenses and do not reduce net operating income.

### Depreciation and acquisition accounting

The Maple Street acquisition includes transaction costs and a
land/building allocation. Land is not depreciated. Building depreciation
is calculated over the assumed useful life, with a partial-year charge
for the acquisition year. Furniture, fixtures and equipment are
depreciated separately.

## Controls and validation

The workbook includes formula-linked accounting schedules and
reconciliation checks designed to test the flow of information across
the accounting cycle. Key validation points include:

-   Debits and credits balance in journal entries.
-   General ledger balances reconcile to the trial balance.
-   The trial balance supports the financial statements.
-   The balance sheet balances.
-   The bank reconciliation reconciles book cash to the adjusted bank
    balance.
-   The cash flow statement reconciles to the movement in cash.
-   Mortgage principal is separated between current and long-term
    presentation.

The workbook should be recalculated in a compatible spreadsheet
application after any changes to assumptions or transactions. Users
should inspect the check cells and supporting schedules rather than
relying only on displayed totals.

## Scope and limitations

This project is a learning and portfolio exercise, not professional
accounting advice. It uses simplified assumptions and fictional data.
Accounting policies, classifications, tax treatments, lease accounting,
lender covenants and reporting requirements can differ by jurisdiction,
entity structure and contract terms. The workbook is not a substitute
for review by a qualified accountant.

## How to review the workbook

1.  Start with **Company Profile & Chart of Accounts** to understand the
    entity, account structure and assumptions.
2.  Follow a sample item from the **Transaction Register** into
    **Journal Entries** and the **General Ledger**.
3.  Review the **Trial Balance** and **Adjusting Entries** to understand
    period-end accounting.
4.  Inspect the **Bank Reconciliation** and the three financial
    statements.
5.  Finish with the **Management Financial Package** for property-level
    and portfolio-level interpretation.
